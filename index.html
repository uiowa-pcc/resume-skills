Stage What the student is trying to do Tool should do Discover “What do I even have to talk about?” Surface experiences Understand “What did I s + evidence Translate “How do I express this here?” Bullet, interview story, cover-letter evidence, etc. Build “Put this into the right document.” Assemble the resume/application Check “Did I do it correctly?” Review formatting/content/tailoring That separation matters. UX guidance consistently recommends breaking complex processes into discrete tasks, showing only what is needed at that moment, and not asking users to provide information twice. And there is a career-development reason to emphasize the first three stages. NACE continues to find that students struggle not simply with having experiences, but with recognizing and articulating how those experiences demonstrate workplace competencies. Its 2024 Student Survey involved more than 20,000 students, and NACE specifically identifies that translation problem as an important gap. More recently, NACE reported that students who understand skills-based hiring are substantially more likely to put skills gained from classes, internships, and co-curricular experiences onto their resumes. So I would rebuild the ecosystem around career evidence, not around resumes. I would simplify the experience tool This is the change from what we've built that I feel most strongly about.
